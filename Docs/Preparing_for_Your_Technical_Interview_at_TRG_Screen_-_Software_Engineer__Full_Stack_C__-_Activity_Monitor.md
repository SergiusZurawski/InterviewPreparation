# Preparing for Your Technical Interview at TRG Screen

**Role:** Senior Software Engineer, Full Stack (C# / .NET) — Activity Monitor
**Version:** November 2026

> Thank you for your interest in joining TRG Screen! This guide explains what to expect from your interview with the Activity Monitor Team and how to prepare.

---

## Format

A **90-minute session** with the Activity Monitor team. It combines:

1. Introductions
2. A **behavioural conversation** about how you work
3. A **hands-on code review exercise** on a pull request written with AI
4. Some **technical questions**

---

## How We Approach Engineering Interviews

TRG Screen is becoming an **AI-driven engineering organization**, and our interviews reflect how engineers actually work today. We're less interested in language trivia or memorized algorithms than in how you combine your engineering expertise with modern tools.

The interview will involve reviewing a sample code change or diff in **C# (.NET)**:

- identifying potential issues,
- discussing improvements,
- explaining how you would resolve them.

You're welcome to use your preferred **AI coding tool** during the exercise. Please **share your screen, including your AI tool**, and **think out loud**.

> ⚠️ Please don't paste anything from your current employer into an AI tool, and make sure none of their code or other projects are visible on your screen.

---

## What We're Looking For

| Area | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Your thought process** | How you break down a problem and talk through your reasoning as you go. |
| **How you give AI context** | The information, constraints and prompts you provide to get useful output. |
| **How you evaluate AI suggestions** | Whether you check them critically or accept them at face value. |
| **Issue spotting** | Catching problems that might otherwise be overlooked: bugs, edge cases, security concerns, performance, readability and test gaps. |
| **Ownership** | Your ability to validate the output and stand behind the final result. |
| **Engineering fundamentals & judgment** | Sound technical decisions and clear communication of the trade-offs. |

> The goal isn't to see how well you can use an AI tool, but **how effectively you pair it with your own expertise**.

---

## How to Prepare

- **Practice reviewing code.** Read pull requests or diffs and look for correctness, edge cases, error handling, security and maintainability. The team works in **C# with ASP.NET Core and EF Core**, so it helps to be comfortable reading that kind of code.
- **Be ready to talk through your AI workflow.** Think of examples where AI helped, where it got things wrong, and how you caught it.
- **Refresh your fundamentals in C# and .NET**, including testing and debugging.
- **Plan to think out loud.** Explaining your reasoning matters as much as the answer.
- **Prepare examples from your own work** for the behavioural conversation, for instance:
  - something you owned end to end,
  - a time requirements were unclear,
  - a time your team changed how it worked.

---

## Technical Setup Checklist

- [ ] A stable internet connection and a quiet space.
- [ ] A working camera and microphone.
- [ ] **Choose how you'll work** — either option is fine. They're interested in *how* you work with AI, not which setup you use:
  - **Option A — IDE:** Your editor with your preferred AI coding tool, installed and logged in. The exercise will be shared as a **markdown file in the call chat**. Open it in a **new, empty folder** so your AI tool can't see other projects.
  - **Option B — Chat-based AI:** A chat-based AI tool in a browser or separate window, with the exercise open in any markdown viewer. You'll copy and paste between the two.
- [ ] If you don't have an AI tool, tell your recruiter beforehand.
- [ ] **Screen-sharing permissions enabled.** You may need to briefly rejoin the call after granting them, so **test beforehand**.
